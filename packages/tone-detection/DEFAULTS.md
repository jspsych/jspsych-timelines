# Tone detection defaults: where they come from and what was changed

The anchor is the online tone-in-noise protocol of Zhao, Brown, Holt & Dick (2022, *Trends in Hearing*, S012), picked by the spec generator (typicality 0.56, coverage 0.77, lead 0.40 over PSYCHOACOUSTICS-WEB, S005; no escalation). Its numbers are in the dossier (`runs/2026-11-12-tone-detection-b02`): a 1000 Hz, 250 ms pure tone with 10 ms raised-cosine ramps in white noise, three intervals 250 ms apart ("which interval contains the tone", keys 1, 2, 3, digits shown), a 3-down/1-up staircase in dB SNR with 1.5 dB steps and then 0.75 dB, starting at -13.75 dB SNR, three 40-trial tracks, a progress counter, six practice trials at -13.8 dB SNR with feedback (no criterion), feedback in practice only, a bonus, a volume-setting screen (the participant sets the device volume until a fixed digital noise is just detectable; the masker is then 40 dB above it), a Huggins-pitch headphone screen (6 trials, 6 correct, two attempts), desktop Chrome only. The threshold is the mean of the last 6 reversals (the mode and a psychometric fit agree within 0.2 dB).

Four sources record the same design in other units (frequency discrimination in Hz or %, S005 and S036; intensity discrimination, S059; constant stimuli, S013 and S059). Only the detection variants are built; the others throw, naming `task_variant`.

## Defaults changed from the spec

| Option | Spec default (basis) | Package default | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| `adaptive.procedure` | `3down1up` (anchor) | `"staircase_1up_3down"` | `3down1up` is not a vocabulary value, and the generated schema's default for it is that string, which its own enum rejects. 3-down/1-up is `staircase_1up_3down`: one wrong answer makes the task easier, three correct answers in a row make it harder (79.4% correct). `staircase_1up_2down` is 70.7%, `staircase_1up_1down` 50% or the target the steps imply. `ascending_discontinue`, `quest` and `rt_based` throw. The stop-signal package reads "1up_3down" the other way round (its level is a delay, where "up" is harder); the vocabulary does not say which. |
| `adaptive.step_size` | `{ scale: dB, steps: ["1.5 dB descent", "0.75 dB"], switch_after: "first error or -19.75 dB SNR" }` | `[1.5, 0.75]` | A list indexed by the number of reversals so far (core's `step_sizes`): 1.5 dB until the first reversal, then 0.75 dB. The first error after a descent is the first reversal, so that half of the rule is exact. **The switch at -19.75 dB SNR is not implemented**: core switches on the number of reversals only. The difference shows only in a track that reaches -19.75 dB without an error (24 correct answers in a row from the start level). The vocabulary's `step_size` is a number or `{ up, down }`; the list is an extension. |
| `adaptive.discontinue_rule` | `{ criterion: "trials", n: 40 }` | `{ trials: 40 }` | Core's shape. `reversals`, `consecutive_failures`, `failures_at_level` and `ceiling` are core's; `mistakes` (S036, S045) is counted by the package. `null` throws: a track needs an end (and a hard cap of 200 trials ends a track whose rule never fires). |
| `adaptive.start_level`, `adaptive.bounds` | -13.75 (anchor); no bounds | -13.75 dB SNR, `[-60, 10]` with a masker; **-40 dB FS, `[-100, -6]`, steps `[3, 1.5]` in quiet** | The anchor has no bounds. These keep the tone above -60 dB SNR and below +10 dB SNR (a masker at -20 dB FS plus a tone at +10 dB SNR does not clip; a bound that would clip throws). The quiet defaults are the package's: no source in the dossier gives a numeric start level in quiet (S031: "participant-set soft but audible"). **Needs human decision.** |
| `adaptive.tracks`, `adaptive.reset` | not in the spec | `"per_condition"`, `"block"` | Every track is its own staircase, started at `start_level` in its own block. `"single"`, `"never"` and `"phase"` would carry a level across conditions or tracks and throw. |
| `presentation_level_db` | none (`+40 dB re the participant's just-detectable noise level`, untyped) | `-20` dB FS (the masker's RMS) | The unit is a plain digital level. S012 set the volume of the device against a fixed digital noise, so nothing is relative at run time: the "+40 dB" is two digital levels, the calibration noise and the masker. -20 dB FS is the package's choice (it leaves room for a tone up to +10 dB SNR). **Needs human decision.** |
| `calibration_level_db` (new) | not in the spec | `presentation_level_db - 40` with a masker (-60 dB FS), -60 dB FS in quiet | The level of the volume-screen sound; the anchor's 40 dB sensation level. The data record it and the masker's level re it (`level_re_calibration_db`). The vocabulary has no field for a level re a calibration sound, and S013 and S044 (`-6` and `+6 dB re the calibration sound`) use one too. **Needs human decision** (proposed: a vocabulary field, or `presentation_level_db` taking `{ level, re }`). |
| `calibration_target` (new) | `volume_calibration: participant_adjusted` for two procedures | `"just_detectable"` (S012) | `comfortable` (S013, S044) is the other. The translations map both to `participant_adjusted` and lose the difference. |
| `practice` | `practice_trials: 6`, feedback in practice only | `{ trial_feedback: "accuracy", adaptive: null }`, fixed levels at the staircase's start level | S012 practised six trials at -13.8 dB SNR, its start level, with feedback ("correct"/"wrong", 1 s). `difficulty_levels` takes the fixed levels; with no list the practice level is the test's `adaptive.start_level`, or the test's own fixed levels. `adaptive` and `difficulty_levels` describe the test, so (like `sequence_constraints`, standard §2a) they reach practice only when given inside `practice`: a top-level staircase never turns six practice trials into a staircase with a 40-trial stop rule. `practice: { adaptive: {...} }` runs a staircase for `practice_trials` trials and ignores its stop rule. (The standard's precedence says a top-level value overrides the package's practice default; for these two options it would not be what anyone means. See the friction notes.) |
| `trial_feedback`, `feedback_condition` | `none` in the test, practice only (anchor); `all` | `"none"`, practice `"accuracy"`; `"all"` | As the anchor. `rt`, `accuracy_rt`, `correct_answer` throw: a trial has no RT worth giving back (the answer waits for the end of the last interval), and the standard asks for none in feedback. |
| `feedback_duration_ms`, `iti_ms` | not in the spec | 1000, 500 | S012's practice feedback lasts 1 s (dossier trial anatomy). The ITI is not in the dossier; 500 ms is the package's choice. |
| `device_eligibility` | `browser_restricted` (anchor: desktop Chrome only) | `"none"` | A device and browser restriction cannot be enforced by a timeline (and excluding a browser by name is not defensible). The values other than `none` throw. |
| `headphone_check`, `headphone_screen` (new) | `screening_task` (anchor: Huggins pitch) | `"screening_task"`, `"antiphase_quietest_tone"` | The spec says only "screening_task". The package makes the screen explicit: the antiphase screen of Woods et al. (2017; S044, S013), six trials; the number correct is recorded on every row and nobody is excluded. S012's Huggins pitch screen needs an FFT-built binaural noise and throws (`huggins_pitch`, `antiphase_plus_NoSpi_chirp`). **Needs human decision:** implement Huggins pitch, or keep the antiphase screen as the default. |
| `participant_eligibility` | `hearing` (fill: 1 of 2 sources) | `"none"` | `hearing` is a self-report question; the answer is recorded (`hearing_check`, `hearing_difficulty`) and nobody is excluded. A default that asks everyone a question the package then ignores is noise, so it is off by default. **Needs human decision.** |
| `environment_instructions` | none (anchor: "turn off notifications", untyped) | `"quiet_room"` | An auditory task needs headphones and a quiet place, and two of the three sources that give an environment instruction (S044, S031) use `quiet_room`. The anchor's notification advice is in the same page. **Needs human decision** (the vocabulary should add `no_interruptions`). |
| `performance_incentive` | `bonus` (anchor) | `"none"` | Payment is the researcher's. `bonus`, `points_only` and `fixed_payment` throw. |
| `tone_frequency_hz` | `{ standard_frequency_hz: 1000, frequency_roving: null }`; lists read as per-block | `[1000]`; a list is a set of conditions | The translations read a list as one frequency per block. With that reading the number of conditions would have to equal the number of blocks, and `trials_per_condition` would have no meaning. Here each frequency is a condition with `trials_per_condition` tracks, in rounds ordered by `block_order`. **Frequency roving is not implemented** (the anchor does not rove). |
| `stimulus_set` | `{ "tone timbre": pure_tone, masker: white_noise }` | `{ tone: "pure_tone", masker: "white_noise" }` | The task's own role names. `masker: "none"` is detection in quiet (S031). Harmonic and inharmonic complexes, instrument notes and threshold-equalizing noise throw. |
| `block_order` | `random_within_block` (fill) | `"random_within_block"` | Each round of tracks holds every condition once, in random order (S036). No effect with one frequency. `fixed`, `ascending`, `descending` are also implemented; `random`, `counterbalanced`, `alternating` throw. |
| `response_keys` | not in the spec (S012: 1, 2, 3) | `["1", "2", "3"]`, or `["1", "2"]` with two intervals | Digits are allowed (not punctuation). One key per interval. |
| `progress_indicator` | `counter` | `"counter"` | "Progress: 12/40" under the stage when the stop rule is a trial count, "Trial 12" otherwise. |
| `instruction_demonstration` | not in the spec | `"interactive"` | PACKAGE-STANDARD §6a, adapted to sound (see the docs page). |
| `practice_criterion` | `null` (anchor) | `null`; when given, `accuracy` defaults to 0.8, `window` to the whole block, `on_fail` to `"repeat"`, `max_repeats` to 2 | S005's lab version advances after 6 correct in a row (the spec's `{ accuracy: 1, window: 6, on_fail: "repeat" }`, untyped here): that is a preset value. |
| `n_test_trials`, `n_blocks`, `trials_per_block` | not in the spec | not offered, except `trials_per_block` for fixed levels | A track ends by its stop rule. Each track is a block; `trials_per_condition` and the frequencies set the number of tracks. |

## Choices the spec leaves open

- **Levels.** One convention: dB re digital full scale as an RMS level (0 dB FS is the RMS of a full-scale sine), SNR as the difference of levels. See the docs page and `LEVEL_UNITS` in `src/constants.ts`. S012's "+40 dB re just-detectable" needs no run-time reference: the participant sets the volume of the device (which the page cannot read) against a fixed digital sound, and the masker is a fixed digital level 40 dB above it.
- **Audio without files.** Sounds are samples computed in plain functions and played as an `AudioBuffer` on jsPsych's audio context. Why not oscillator and noise nodes: they need a running context to test anything and give noise no exact level. Why not WAV files for the audio plugins: the plugins have no onset marker for the intervals, and the sound has to be made from options anyway (`utils.audio.toWav` renders the same samples to a WAV file for anyone who wants one). Data-only simulation and jest (jsdom, no AudioContext) need no audio: the sound is made in `on_load`, which they never run, and the player does nothing without a context and says so (`audio_context_state: "none"`).
- **The masker is continuous through each trial, not through the experiment** (S012 loops a 300 s noise). Gapless audio across jsPsych trials (the dossier's `masker_gating`, `requires: plugin-change`) is not available: each trial's sound is one buffer, the masker running from 300 ms before the first interval to the end of the last, faded at both ends. Gated maskers (S044) are the same with a burst per interval, not implemented.
- **Timing of the markers.** The sound starts 50 ms after the listening screen and the interval markers light at the planned times (`setTimeout`), so they can differ from the audio by a few ms. `rt` is measured from the answer screen, which starts when the listening screen's timer ends (the end of the last interval, to the timer's accuracy). The browser's audio output latency was not measured.
- **Threshold.** The score is the mean of the last 6 reversals of each track (S005, S012, S044), averaged over the tracks of a condition; `last_n_reversals` (4 in S036 and S045) is an option of `calculateScores`. The mode of the track's levels (S012) and the final level (S031) are reported next to it. Nothing is excluded: the criteria are listed on the docs page.
- **Reversals.** Core counts a reversal when a move changes direction, whichever way (an up move after a down move, or a down move after an up move), and the level of a reversal is the level presented on that trial. A track's first move is never a reversal.
- **Clipping.** A masker plus the loudest tone that the bounds allow must fit in full scale (noise peaks counted at 4.5 SD) or `createTimeline` throws; a sound that would clip anyway (a custom level) is scaled down as a whole and the row says so (`sound_limited`).
- **Walkthrough.** A listening example (replay as often as wanted), then two check steps (intervals 3 and 1 of 3; 2 and 1 of 2) at a clearly audible level (6 dB above the masker; 36 dB above the calibration sound in quiet), with a hint, replay and three attempts. See the docs page for what in §6a did not fit sound.
- **No exclusion.** The headphone screen, the hearing question and the practice have no gate. `practice_criterion` is allowed and defaults to `null`.

## Spec defaults kept, with the coherence check

| Option | Default | Source | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| `inter_item_interval_ms`, `stimulus_duration_ms`, `tone_ramp_ms` | 250, 250, 10 | S012 | Two 250 ms gaps and three 250 ms intervals: the three-interval sound is 1.55 s with the 300 ms lead-in. 2 x 10 ms ramps fit inside 250 ms. |
| `task_variant`, `judgment_wording` | `3I-3AFC_which_contains_tone`, `which_interval_contains_tone` | S012 | `2I-2AFC_which_contains_tone` (S044) is also implemented. |
| `trials_per_condition` | 3 | S012 | Three 40-trial tracks: 120 test trials, 1.55 s of sound each. |
| `practice_trials` | 6 | S012 | Six trials at the start level. |
| `headphone_check_trials` | 6 | S012, S013 | |

## Quick demo

The Quick demo keeps the default instructions, the volume screen and the walkthrough. It sets `practice_trials: 4`, `headphone_check_trials: 3` and `trials_per_condition: 1`, and **also `adaptive`: the track starts at -15 dB SNR, takes 3 dB then 1.5 dB steps and ends after 20 trials.** The standard's quick demo "sets only the vocabulary's count options"; a staircase's length is the stop rule (`adaptive.discontinue_rule`), and a track from the anchor's start would hardly reach a reversal in 20 trials, so the demo also moves the start level nearer the threshold and enlarges the steps. Measured with a scripted participant in headless Chrome (reading each instruction page for 2.5 s, answering each trial about 0.8 s after the answer screen, 90% correct): **126 s and 130 s in two runs (about 2 min 10 s)**: 24 s of instruction pages, 2.7 s volume screen, 19 s headphone screen, 20 s of walkthrough and practice, 60 s for the 20 test trials, 2.5 s end screen (the sounds are 1.55 s per trial). The track reversed once or twice in 20 trials for a 90%-correct scripted participant, and more for a listener whose threshold is nearer the start level. All 62 sound rows of the second run recorded `audio_context_state: "running"`.

**In the docs demo's sandbox.** The same package, run inside a sandboxed iframe with `sandbox="allow-scripts"` and the page in a `srcdoc` (the theme's `TimelineExample` setup), in headless Chrome with clicks as the only gesture: every sound row recorded `"running"`. The first click on an instruction page resumes the audio context (the pages ask jsPsych for it when they finish), so the first sound plays. No change to the theme is needed. Not checked: real loudspeakers or headphones, other browsers, mobile.
